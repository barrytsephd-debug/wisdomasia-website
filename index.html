01 / About WisdomAsia

Frozen foods · Greater China

From colour to crystal

A leading multinational’s frozen dumplings were losing ground to brightly coloured rivals. How could it innovate and win back leadership?

Read the case

The question

A leading multinational’s frozen glutinous rice dumplings were losing ground to rivals selling brightly coloured versions made with vegetable and fruit juices. How could it innovate and take back market leadership?

What we found

Ethnographic research showed the appeal was the colour, not the juices. The added flavours could even count against the product, interfering with the taste people expect from a dumpling.

What we recommended

A translucent wrapper that lets the filling show its own colour. It offered visual appeal without artificial colouring or unexpected flavours, and seeing exactly what’s inside signalled authenticity. When R&D judged it technically unfeasible, we pointed them to how Cantonese dim sum chefs make crystal dumpling skins.

The outcome

A distinctive translucent range that the brand launched successfully.

Hospitality · Mainland China

Designing for the traveller in between

An international hotel group was bringing a limited-service brand into China. Who should it serve, and what should it offer them?

Read the case

The question

An international hotel group was bringing a limited-service brand into China. Who should it be built for, and how should its rooms and services reflect what those guests actually need?

What we found

Quantitative profiling of hotel users and analysis of how they chose hotels pinpointed an underserved group: frequent mid-level business travellers. Without the corporate perks that put senior staff in four- and five-star hotels, they had to choose between unpredictable independent hotels and the basic standard of domestic budget chains.

What we recommended

Testing in mock rooms with these travellers surfaced small but decisive details. Female guests needed handheld shower heads, to wash without wetting long hair and for personal care. Breakfast was simplified and leaned Western (toast, coffee, baked beans, hash browns) rather than the congee, boiled eggs and steamed buns of domestic chains: quick and easy for busy managers under 40 travelling for meetings.

The outcome

The first hotels were an immediate success, and the rollout to further cities confirmed the direction.

Destination marketing · Mainland China

Pure by comparison

A national promotion agency needed one positioning that would attract Chinese tourists, buyers and students alike, and set the country apart from its neighbour.

Read the case

The question

A national promotion agency wanted to find the claim that was uniquely its country’s own and also appealed to Chinese audiences, not only for tourism but for trade and international education.

What we found

Quantitative and qualitative research showed that, in Chinese eyes, the country’s natural landscape and the purity of its environment were its biggest assets. That image carried over into what Chinese audiences expected it to be good at, both in the products it exports and in the subjects it teaches. But the same research placed it very close to a neighbouring country, so nature alone would not set it apart.

What we recommended

Lead on purity, where the country is seen as relatively more untouched than its neighbour, and go further with authentic cultural and nature-focused experiences.

The outcome

The research confirmed purity as the claim to lead with for Chinese audiences and sharpened how to stand apart. The country’s promotion in China continues to centre on untouched nature, extended with people and cultural experiences.

04 / Our philosophy. Our reason for being.

Transcultural
Intelligence

Data can tell us what people say and do. Cultural understanding helps us interpret what it means.

Language, values, social expectations and everyday experience shape how people understand brands and respond to communications. The same message can carry different meanings across markets.

We bring research evidence and cultural context together, translating local understanding into insight that organisations can use. This is the thinking behind WisdomAsia, and the meaning of our longstanding tagline.

Our approach

Beyond the data.
Towards understanding.

Sound research starts with a clear question and ends with insight that can be used.

How this shapes our work ↗

Since 2004, WisdomAsia has built a core research team that has now worked together on projects for more than fifteen years. Our researchers bring senior experience in qualitative moderation, quantitative design and analysis, and project management, with backgrounds at leading international research agencies and on the client side.

Each assignment is staffed from this team according to the question at hand, so clients work with experienced researchers who share our methods, our standards and a deep understanding of Chinese consumers.

Research direction

WisdomAsia’s research direction is set by its founder, Dr Barry Tse, who combines more than thirty years of experience in marketing research with a PhD in Psychology. His career began at Nielsen and Synovate and includes six years on the Board of WIN, and his doctoral research focused on how constructs are understood and measured across cultures. He challenges the team to frame questions sharply and to interpret findings with cultural and analytical depth.
